## 2. The solicitor email

```
Subject: One question on our corporate objects and holding customer funds

Good day,

You handled the incorporation of VALLO SPACES LTD, RC 9870413, and you will remember that our first draft objects clause was recouched after the CAC query, because including payment, escrow and wallet wording raised the minimum share capital to 500,000,000 naira. We incorporated with 1,000,000 naira share capital and those words were removed.

We are building a property marketplace and I need to know what we can lawfully do as currently incorporated. Two specific things:

1. We want users to hold a naira balance with us that they fund by card through Paystack, and spend on the platform.

2. We want to hold a buyer's or tenant's payment until both sides confirm, and then release it to the seller or landlord. An escrow, in plain terms.

My questions:

a. Can we do either of those as currently incorporated, given the objects clause as it now stands?

b. If not, what is the cheapest lawful structure? I understand the options are roughly: a bank or trustee holding the funds with us never touching them, or acting as agent for a licensed payment provider who is the principal. Which do you recommend for a company of our size, and what would it cost us to set up?

c. Does the Central Bank of Nigeria's licensing regime treat a marketplace holding customer balances as an activity reserved to licensed operators, and if so which category?

d. If we do proceed, what must our terms of service say, and what must our accountant do to keep customer funds segregated from company revenue?

We have not taken a single naira into either arrangement yet, so nothing is at risk today and I would rather ask now than fix it later.

Thank you.
```
