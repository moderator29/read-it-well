## 2. The Paystack compliance question, which goes first because it is free

```
Subject: Can a marketplace hold customer balances and hold funds in escrow on Paystack?

Hello,

We are VALLO SPACES LTD, RC 9870413, a property and stays marketplace in Nigeria. We are integrating Paystack now and I want to get the money design right before we take a single live payment, rather than after.

Two questions about what is permitted on your rails and under your compliance rules.

1. Can a marketplace on Paystack hold a naira balance on behalf of its users? The user funds it by card and spends it inside our platform. If that is not permitted, what do merchants in our position normally do instead?

2. Can we hold a buyer's or tenant's payment until both sides confirm, and then release it to the landlord or seller? I know some marketplaces do this with split payments, subaccounts or delayed settlement rather than by holding the money themselves. Which of your features is the right one for that, and is there anything we are not allowed to describe to our users as escrow?

Background so you can answer precisely: our CAC objects clause does not include payment, escrow or wallet wording, because including it would have required 500,000,000 naira in share capital. So we are looking for the structure where the funds never sit with us.

Thank you.
```

## 3. The lawyer email, if Paystack's answer leaves the licensing question open

```
Subject: One question on our corporate objects and holding customer funds

Good day,

VALLO SPACES LTD, RC 9870413, incorporated 18 September 2026. Our first draft objects clause was recouched after the CAC query, because including payment, escrow and wallet wording raised the minimum share capital to 500,000,000 naira. We incorporated with 1,000,000 naira share capital and those words were removed.

We are building a property marketplace and I need to know what we can lawfully do as currently incorporated. Two specific things:

1. Users hold a naira balance with us that they fund by card through Paystack and spend on the platform.

2. We hold a buyer's or tenant's payment until both sides confirm, then release it to the seller or landlord. An escrow, in plain terms.

My questions:

a. Can we do either of those as currently incorporated, given the objects clause as it now stands?

b. If not, what is the cheapest lawful structure? My understanding is the options are roughly: a bank or trustee holding the funds with us never touching them, or acting as agent for a licensed payment provider who is the principal, or designing it so the money is split at the payment processor and never reaches our account at all. Which do you recommend for a company of our size, and what would it cost to set up?

c. Does the Central Bank of Nigeria's licensing regime treat a marketplace holding customer balances as an activity reserved to licensed operators, and if so which category?

d. If we proceed, what must our terms of service say, and what must our accountant do to keep customer funds segregated from company revenue?

We have not taken a single naira into either arrangement yet, so nothing is at risk today and I would rather ask now than fix it later. If this is outside your practice area, I would be grateful for a referral to somebody who does fintech regulatory work.

Thank you.
```
