## Speed and fewer taps

### B1. Messages get a thumb: an unread count in the dock, and a slot ruling

- **Problem.** Messages is the daily loop of a marketplace. On a phone it sits
  behind the sixth slot: `components/app/MobileTabBar.tsx` builds the five
  slots from Home, Search, the "+", Feed and Profile (lines 291 to 336), and
  Messages is the first row of the `DockMore` tray (line 349). No
  unread-message count exists anywhere in the chrome: a search for
  `unreadMessages`, `unreadThreads` or `messagesUnread` in `components/app`
  finds nothing. The only mark is the bell's dot, which mixes a new message
  with a listing approval and a review. So a renter waiting on an agent's reply
  has to open More, then Messages, to find out if anyone answered. That is two
  taps on every check, many times a day.
- **Proposal.**
  1. Read one figure, the count of threads with unread messages, in
     `getShellIdentity` beside `unreadNotifications`. It costs one head count
     under RLS.
  2. On the More button, a 4px brand dot when the figure is above zero (the
     same dot grammar as the active tab, no container). Inside the tray, the
     Messages row carries the `count` badge from `StatusBadge`. The accessible
     name of the More button says "More, 2 unread conversations".
  3. On the rail (1024 and up), the Messages row carries the same count.
  4. For the founder: promote Messages into the capsule in place of Feed, and
     move Feed into the tray. Airbnb, Jiji and every WhatsApp-trained user
     expect the inbox one tap away. Feed would keep its own entry points (the
     story rail on Home and the Around card).
- **Impact.** It turns the most frequent check in the app from two taps and a
  scan into a glance. Replies get read sooner, so agents see faster responses
  and fewer enquiries go cold.
- **Effort** S for 1 to 3. S for 4 once it is decided. **Priority** P1.
- **Founder decision.** Whether Messages replaces Feed in the capsule (the
  22 September ruling put Feed there). Steps 1 to 3 need no decision.

### B2. Pick up where you left off: surface the searches and listings the app already remembers

- **Problem.** `lib/search/memory.ts` writes the last five searches
  (`rememberSearch`, `RECENT_SEARCHES_KEY`) from
  `components/app/search/SearchMemory.tsx`, and the last eight listings opened
  (`rememberListing`) from `components/app/listing/RecordVisit.tsx`. It also
  exports readers: `recentSearchesSnapshot`, `recentListingsSnapshot` and
  `subscribeRecent`. **Nothing in the app reads them.** The only importers of
  `lib/search/memory` are the two writers. So a renter who searched "2 bed,
  Yaba, under N3m" last night starts from an empty field this morning, and the
  flat they looked at three times is found again only through the back stack.
- **Proposal.**
  1. When the search field on `/search`, `/stays/search` or the Home hero
     gains focus with nothing typed, open a "Recent" list under it. It shows up
     to five searches as rows (the server-built `label`, which already
     describes only what the page ran), and each row reruns its `href` in one
     tap. It ends with a quiet "Clear" that calls `clearRecentSearches`.
  2. On Home, under the hero band, add a "Looked at recently" row of up to
     eight small cards from `recentListingsSnapshot`. It is hidden when empty,
     and each card keeps the Example badge. This sits beside plan item 15's
     "Up next" and does not replace it.
  3. Both read on the device only, and are forgotten on sign out (the
     `ForgetOnSignOut` pattern in `components/app/offline/`).
- **Impact.** The most common second session ("show me that again") takes one
  tap. The data is already collected and costs no network, so the gain comes
  almost free.
- **Effort** S. **Priority** P1.
- **Founder decision.** None.

### B3. Compare what you saved, or stop promising it

- **Problem.** The Saved page promises a feature that does not exist. The
  empty state says "Tap the heart on any card and it waits for you here, ready
  to compare side by side." and the meta strip over the grid reads "Ready to
  compare" (`app/(app)/saved/SavedBoard.tsx`, lines 256 and 282). There is no
  compare view anywhere in `app/(app)` or `components/app`. The claims lint
  catches safety and verification claims, but not a promised feature. Renters in
  Lagos decide between two or three flats on fees, caution and commute. Today
  they do it by flicking between tabs.
- **Proposal.**
  1. **Now, one line.** Change both strings to what is true ("Tap the heart on
     any card and it waits for you here." and "Your shortlist").
  2. **Then the feature.** In Saved, a "Compare" quiet button appears at two
     saves or more. Choosing two or three cards opens a sheet (a two-pane page
     on desktop) with one row per fact, each from the listing's own builders:
     move-in total and its lines (`move-in-lines.ts`), rent period, beds and
     baths, verified or Example, last let (`LastLetLine`), still available
     (`StillAvailable`), the commute band when an anchor is set
     (`lib/listings/commute.ts`), and the photo. A fact the lister did not
     state reads "Not stated", never zero. The lowest figure in a row is
     marked with a dot, not a colour.
  3. Extend the claims lint with a short list of feature phrases ("compare
     side by side", "instant", "real-time") that fail unless a named component
     exists.
- **Impact.** It removes a false promise today. Built, it is the tool that
  turns a shortlist into a decision, which is the moment before an inspection
  request.
- **Effort** S for 1. M for 2. S for 3. **Priority** P1 (step 1 is a claim
  fix).
- **Founder decision.** None for 1. For 2, whether compare is renter-only or
  also covers stays.

### B4. The listing opens in one frame: paint the card's facts first

- **Problem.** The perf sweep measured search to listing at a median of
  3,354ms, with the listing's LCP at 2.7s on the hero photo. Its open items
  point at the image optimiser's cold cache. Whatever the server does, the
  person tapped a card that already held the photo URL, title, area, price and
  badges. The listing route still starts from `loading.tsx`, which is a
  skeleton that knows none of that. On a mid-range Android on 3G that is three
  seconds of grey after a tap.
- **Proposal.** Hand the card's own facts across the navigation and paint them
  at once.
  1. At click time `ListingCard.tsx` already names the photo for the morph
     (motion sweep, "Listing morph"). Also write the card's facts (id, photo
     URL at the card's size, title, area, price line, badge) into a small
     in-memory map keyed by listing id.
  2. `listing/[id]/loading.tsx` becomes a client shell that looks the id up in
     that map. With a hit, it renders the gallery's lead pane from the card
     photo (already in the browser cache, so no second download) plus the
     title block and price line. With a miss, it falls back to the current
     skeleton.
  3. The real page streams in over it with no layout shift, because the lead
     pane and title block keep their sizes.
- **Impact.** The perceived wait drops from about 3.4s to one frame on warm
  taps from search, home, saved and the map dock. That is the single most
  repeated navigation in the app. It helps low-end phones the most, and it
  moves no figure the server has not already printed on the card.
- **Effort** M. **Priority** P1.
- **Founder decision.** None. Coordinate with whoever owns `loading.tsx` files
  (lane B in the last plan).

## Viewings, deals and money (UX only)

### B5. The viewing day kit: calendar, reminders, and "running late" in one tap

- **Problem.** An inspection in Lagos is a trip across traffic to meet a
  stranger at a gate. The product carries the gate part well: the offline
  inspection pack (`lib/offline/pack.ts`), the handshake
  (`GateHandshake.tsx`), safety share (`SafetyShareControl.tsx`) and the maps
  link (`ExactPlace.tsx`). What happens before the gate is missing.
  - There is no calendar export: `.ics`, `text/calendar` and `addToCalendar`
    have no hits.
  - There is no reminder: `lib/push` has no viewing or inspection reminder,
    and `grep -i remind` over `components/app/inspections` and `plans` finds
    nothing.
  - "I am on my way" or "running 20 minutes late" is typed by hand into the
    thread, usually while the person is on an okada or in a bus.
- **Proposal.** On a CONFIRMED inspection (the row in `InspectionsBoard`, the
  thread's inspection card and the Plans "Coming up" row):
  1. **Add to calendar.** It builds an `.ics` on the device. The title is
     "Viewing: {area}". The location is the area and state only (the pack's own
     rule: no street address in an artefact that leaves the app). There is a
     link back to the inspection, and a 90 minute alarm.
  2. **Two reminders** through the existing push and WhatsApp junction
     (`lib/notify/junction.ts`): at 7pm the evening before, and two hours
     before the slot. Both are in the "bookings" topic and so respect quiet
     hours. Each names the time, the area and who is showing it, and nothing
     else.
  3. **On the day**, the card shows a "Day of" strip with four quiet
     actions: Directions (the `ExactPlace` link, once the address is released),
     Share my trip (`SafetyShareControl`), "On my way" and "Running late". The
     last two send a fixed, prewritten message into the thread through the
     outbox, so it works with a weak signal. "Running late" asks 15, 30 or 60
     minutes and nothing else.
- **Impact.** Fewer missed viewings on both sides, fewer "where are you?"
  calls, and a visible reason to keep the conversation on Vallo rather than
  moving to a phone call. Agents feel it as fewer no-shows.
- **Effort** M. **Priority** P1.
- **Founder decision.** The reminder times (default: 7pm the evening before,
  and two hours before), and whether the reminders also go to the lister.

### B6. Renter question chips on the first message, skipping what the listing already answers

- **Problem.** The lister has a quick-reply tray worded from the listing's own
  facts (`lib/enquiry/quick-replies.ts`, V-72). The renter gets a placeholder
  only: "Say hello, and ask what you want to know. Is it still available? Can
  you do an inspection this week?" (`app/(app)/messages/new/FirstMessage.tsx`,
  line 51). The questions Nigerian renters actually send are predictable:
  - Is the caution refundable?
  - Is it a prepaid meter?
  - Is there water, and a borehole?
  - Is there a generator, and what is the service charge?
  - Is it one year or two years upfront?
  - Can I inspect on Saturday?

  Typing them on a phone is slow, and asking something the listing already
  states makes the renter look careless to the agent.
- **Proposal.** Above the composer on `/messages/new` and on an empty listing
  thread, show a wrapping row of up to five question chips. A tap adds the
  sentence to the draft (it does not send), so the renter can send several at
  once. The chips come from a pure `renterQuestions(listing)` beside
  `quick-replies.ts`. **A question is left out when the listing already
  answers it**: utilities (`ListingUtilities`), caution (`CautionRecordLine`),
  tenure and the shortest term (`ListingTenure`) and viewing windows
  (`ViewingSlots`). Each chip's text is an i18n key in all four locales.
- **Impact.** Better first messages, which get faster and fuller replies. It
  also quietly teaches renters what the listing already says.
- **Effort** S. **Priority** P2.
- **Founder decision.** None.

### B7. An honest reply-time line for listers, printed only when the record supports it

- **Problem.** Ghosting is the top complaint about Nigerian property agents.
  The listing detail shows many trust facts (`VerifiedAgentBadge`,
  `ListerRoleLine`, `LastLetLine`, `ProofStrip`), but nothing about whether this
  person answers. "Responds", "response time" and "usually replies" have no
  hits in `components/app` or `app/(app)`. Airbnb's "Typically responds within
  an hour" is one of its strongest conversion lines, because it is measured.
- **Proposal.**
  1. A nightly database function computes, per lister, the median time from a
     conversation's first renter message to the lister's first reply, over
     the last 90 days.
  2. It publishes a band ("within an hour", "within a few hours", "within a
     day") **only when there are at least 8 such conversations**. Otherwise it
     publishes nothing, and the line is absent rather than "New".
  3. It is shown as one muted line under the agent card on the listing
     (`ListingAgentCard.tsx`) and in the first-message screen: "Usually
     replies within a few hours". It carries a small info link that says how it
     is measured.
  4. The lister sees their own band, and what would move it, in their
     workspace.
- **Impact.** Renters message the listers who answer, and listers now have a
  reason to answer. It is a trust signal built from behaviour, not from a
  badge.
- **Effort** M. **Priority** P2.
- **Founder decision.** The minimum sample (default 8), and whether a slow band
  ("more than a day") is shown or simply absent (default: absent).

### B8. Price context on the listing: "How does this price compare?"

- **Problem.** Price Check is one of the most distinctive tools in the product,
  and it accepts every input as a query parameter: `state`, `lga`, `area`,
  `lat`, `lng`, `type`, `intent`, `period`, `beds` and `size`
  (`app/(app)/price/page.tsx`, the reads after line 90). But no listing, stay
  or saved card links to it. `grep` for `/price` or `priceCheck` over
  `components/app/listing` and `app/(app)/listing` finds nothing. The renter
  has to leave the listing, open the More tray, find Price Check and retype the
  area, type and beds.
- **Proposal.** On the listing detail, under the move-in summary card, add one
  quiet row: "How does this price compare?". It deep-links to `/price` with the
  listing's own area, coordinates, type, intent, period and beds filled in, so
  the answer arrives with the HTML (the page already computes it on the
  server). Where Price Check refuses, it refuses there, with its own honest
  "Not enough to tell". Nothing about the result is painted on the listing
  itself, so no band is ever shown beside a price the tool did not judge. On
  an Example listing the row is hidden, because comparables exclude
  `is_demo`.
- **Impact.** It turns a separate tool into part of the decision, at the moment
  of doubt, with no new claim.
- **Effort** S. **Priority** P2.
- **Founder decision.** None. The copy must keep the "not a valuation" rule
  (`scripts/check-valuation-words.mjs`).

### B9. Agreements show what changed before you confirm again

- **Problem.** "Changing anything makes the version move and every
  confirmation lapse" (`app/(app)/agreements/[id]/page.tsx`, the comment at
  line 74). That rule is right, but the person asked to confirm again cannot
  see what moved. The history reads "Terms changed" (`EVENT_LABEL.amended`),
  and `deal_agreement_events` stores `terms_version` with no snapshot of the
  terms. So a tenant who already agreed N2.5m rent, N250k caution and 10%
  agency is sent the whole sheet again. They must reread every line of a
  document about the largest sum they will move this year, looking for the one
  that changed. That is exactly where a bad actor would hide a change.
- **Proposal.**
  1. Keep an immutable snapshot of the terms per version (a
     `deal_agreement_versions` table, written by the same function that bumps
     the version).
  2. On an agreement whose current version is newer than the one this party
     last confirmed, open with a tinted "What changed since you confirmed"
     card. It lists each changed line with the old figure struck through and
     the new one beside it, as rows from the same `LINES` table the page
     already uses. It names who changed it and when.
  3. The confirm panel (plan item 22) repeats the changed lines in its
     itemised block, so the person confirms the difference, not a wall.
- **Impact.** It closes the easiest route to a quiet price change, makes
  re-confirming fast, and is the kind of transparency a Revolut user expects
  from anything about money.
- **Effort** M (one migration, one card). **Priority** P1.
- **Founder decision.** None, beyond confirming that version snapshots may be
  kept for the retention period in `docs/RETENTION_SCHEDULE.md`.

### B10. Rent countdown for sitting tenants: when it is due, and what setting aside looks like

- **Problem.** Nigerian rent is paid a year (often two) upfront, so the renewal
  date is the biggest financial cliff in a tenant's year. The lister side has a
  renewal clock (`components/app/tenancy/RenewalControls.tsx`, V-93, offers
  from 90 days out). The tenant side has no countdown and no nudge until an
  offer arrives. By then, a tenant who has not been setting money aside has 90
  days to find a year's rent.
- **Proposal.** On the tenancy page and in Plans, a tenant with an active
  tenancy sees a summary card: "Rent due in 214 days". The figure is the
  current rent from the tenancy record, and one line of arithmetic below it:
  "About N357,000 a month from now until then". There are reminders at 180, 90,
  30 and 7 days through the bookings topic. There is no wallet, no pot and no
  "save" button. It is a calendar and a division, and the card carries the
  existing `NO_CUSTODY_SENTENCE` wording where money is mentioned. When the
  lister has offered a renewal figure, the card switches to that figure and
  says so.
- **Impact.** Fewer renewal defaults and fewer painful surprises. A reason to
  open Vallo in the ten months between moving in and renewing, which is when
  most marketplaces lose the tenant.
- **Effort** M. **Priority** P2.
- **Founder decision.** Yes. Confirm that a set-aside figure is acceptable
  with no savings product behind it, and the reminder schedule. Any future
  "save towards rent" feature is a regulated product and is out of scope here.

## Notifications and trust

### B11. Notifications that say what needs you: severity, a "Needs you" group, bundles and inline actions

- **Problem.** The notification list is split into New and Earlier
  (`app/(app)/notifications/LiveNotifications.tsx`, lines 199 to 204), with
  one glyph per kind. `lib/push/preferences.ts` says it outright: rows "carry a
  kind and no severity" (the note above `URGENT_KINDS`). So "Your agreement is
  waiting for your confirmation" and "Tunde liked your post" have the same
  weight. Five messages from one thread are five rows. Every action needs a tap
  in, then a second tap on the record.
- **Proposal.**
  1. Add a `severity` column (`action`, `update`, `fyi`) to the notification
     event, set by each builder in `lib/notify`. That is the fix the file
     itself proposes. Quiet hours then read severity, not only `wallet`.
  2. The list opens with a "Needs you" group: only `action` rows that are still
     open (an agreement to confirm, a booking to pay before its hold ends, an
     inspection to accept). Each has one inline primary that goes straight to
     the control ("Confirm", "Pay by Thu 14:20", "Reply"). A row leaves the
     group when the record says it is done, not when it is read.
  3. Bundle rows by record: "3 new messages from Tunde about the Yaba flat" is
     one row that expands.
  4. Push titles use the same severity. An `action` push leads with the verb
     and the deadline, and an `fyi` never wakes the phone.
- **Impact.** The bell becomes a to-do list, not a feed. Deadlines stop being
  missed (a lapsed hold, an unconfirmed agreement), and people keep push on
  because it is worth keeping.
- **Effort** M. **Priority** P1.
- **Founder decision.** The mapping of each notification kind to a severity
  (a one-page table to sign off).

#### B11 severity table: DRAFT FOR THE FOUNDER'S SIGN-OFF (built 30 September)

Built with these defaults while the table waits for a signature. One table,
in two places kept in step: `apps/web/src/lib/notify/severity.ts` (the app)
and `public.notification_severity` (pending migration
`supabase/migrations/pending/20261001090000_m1_b11_b13_severity_and_saved_changes.sql`).
Rules are read top to bottom; the first match wins.

| # | What arrives | Severity | Inline verb |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A new sign-in to your account (`/settings/devices/alert`) | action | Check |
| 2 | A new message in a conversation | action | Reply |
| 3 | Confirm who you are letting for | action | Confirm |
| 4 | A new booking or table request (host) | action | Review |
| 5 | An application needs more information | action | Add details |
| 6 | A withdrawal or payment could not complete | action | Check |
| 7 | Your mandate is running out | action | Renew |
| 8 | You were invited to share a move-in | action | Open |
| 9 | A held payment is paused | action | Open |
| 10 | A new support ticket (staff) | action | Open |
| 11 | Likes, reposts, new followers, badges | fyi | none |
| 12 | Your post, story, event, bio or comment is live or being looked at | fyi | none |
| 13 | "We have your question", "Booking request sent" | fyi | none |
| 14 | Anything else from the feed | fyi | none |
| 15 | Any other message | action | Reply |
| 16 | Everything else (confirmations, cancellations, rent and payment receipts, registrations filed or approved, reviews, price and availability changes on saved places, staff notices) | update | none |

What each severity does today:

- **action**: the "Needs you" group at the top of Notifications, with the verb
  as a button on the row. A message row stays there while its conversation
  still holds an unread message (read or not); any other action leaves when
  it is opened. After 14 days an action is history.
- **update**: New while unread, then Earlier.
- **fyi**: the same list, and its push arrives silent (never wakes the phone).
- Rows about one conversation or one post fold into one row that opens.

Questions for the sign-off: should "Booking confirmed" be an action (pay by a
deadline) where a hold clock exists; should a staff "new sign-in from a new
place" be an action for the staff member; should price changes on saved
places be fyi rather than update.

### B12. A scam shield for the message you receive, not only the one you write

- **Problem.** The safety moment in a thread fires on the renter's own draft
  (`ThreadView.tsx`, `onDraftChange`, lines 415 to 429, using `MONEY_TALK_RE`
  from `lib/messages/education.ts`). But the classic Nigerian rental scam goes
  the other way. The "agent" writes "pay the inspection fee into this account:
  0123456789, GTB" or "send the caution to my personal account to hold it". An
  incoming message with a 10-digit NUBAN, a bank name or "pay into" gets no
  warning at all. `MONEY_TALK_RE` is used in exactly one place, on the draft.
- **Proposal.**
  1. A pure `offPlatformAsk(body)` in `lib/messages/`. It matches an account
     number shape (10 digits), a Nigerian bank name, a payment-app handle, or
     "pay/transfer/send ... to/into" together with a money word. A test pins
     true and false examples.
  2. When a message from the other side matches, render a tinted row under
     that bubble: "Vallo never asks you to pay into a personal account.
     Inspections on Vallo are free." (the existing `NO_INSPECTION_FEE` and
     `OFF_PLATFORM_SENTENCE` constants, not new claims). Add two quiet actions:
     "Report" (the existing `ReportSheet`) and "What to do". It is shown to the
     reader only, and the sender is not told.
  3. The match is logged as a moderation signal (count only) so the trust
     team can see listers who ask repeatedly.
- **Impact.** It protects renters at the exact moment money is requested
  outside the platform. That moment is where Vallo's promise either holds or
  is lost.
- **Effort** S for 1 and 2. S for 3. **Priority** P1.
- **Founder decision.** The wording, and whether step 3 may feed an automatic
  review queue (default: a count only, reviewed by a person).

### B13. Changes on things you saved: price, availability and new viewing windows

- **Problem.** A save is a silent bookmark. Saved searches can alert
  (`components/app/saved-searches/SaveSearchControl.tsx`, "Alerts on"), but a
  saved listing never tells you anything. "Price drop" has no hits across the
  codebase, and saved cards carry no change mark. Renters discover a flat was
  let only when they message about it.
- **Proposal.**
  1. Record listing price and status changes as events (a small
     `listing_changes` table written by the listing update path).
  2. On each Saved card, show one muted change line when something moved since
     the person last opened Saved: "Price changed from N3m to N2.7m", "No
     longer available", or "New viewing windows added". The "since last
     opened" logic follows the `LastVisit.tsx` pattern.
  3. An opt-in push per saved listing, in the "listings" topic, for price and
     availability only. It is off by default, and offered once through the
     `settings_opened` moment or right after a save.
  4. A let or withdrawn listing stays in Saved, greyed, with "See similar
     nearby" (`ListingNeighbours`), rather than vanishing.
- **Impact.** Saves become worth making. It is a strong, non-spammy reason to
  come back, and the renter no longer wastes a message on a flat that is gone.
- **Effort** M. **Priority** P2.
- **Founder decision.** Whether price decreases may be pushed (default:
  in-app only, push on opt-in).

## Feel, access and reach

### B14. One haptic grammar everywhere, used where it counts

- **Problem.** `lib/ui/feedback.ts` defines a careful five-kind grammar
  (select, confirm, success, warning, error) that fires only after an action
  is accepted. In the member app it is used in `ResultSheet.tsx`,
  `GateHandshake.tsx`, `Segmented.tsx` and `Button.tsx`, and nowhere else. A
  second, older path is still live: `ListingActions.tsx` line 238 calls
  `nativeHaptic("success")` when a listing is saved, which breaks the grammar
  (a save is a confirm, not a settled payment). Meanwhile the card heart
  (`SaveControl.tsx`), the swipe-to-remove threshold in
  `app/(app)/saved/SwipeToRemove.tsx`, a message leaving the outbox, dock tab
  changes and the More tray have no feedback at all.
- **Proposal.** Route every haptic in the member app through `feedback()` and
  retire the direct `nativeHaptic` calls. Map each moment to a kind:
  - `select`: a dock tab, the More tray opening, a filter chip, a segmented
    control.
  - `confirm`: a save accepted by the server, a message delivered (on the
    outbox's `OUTBOX_SENT_EVENT`, not on the tap), an inspection request
    accepted, and swipe-to-remove crossing its threshold.
  - `warning`: a message that failed and was kept.
  - `success` stays for money and confirmed viewings only.

  Add a source test that fails on any `nativeHaptic(` outside
  `lib/ui/feedback.ts`.
- **Impact.** The Android and shell app feel physical and consistent, like
  Revolut or iOS itself, and "success" keeps its meaning.
- **Effort** S. **Priority** P3.
- **Founder decision.** None.

### B15. Money and units that a screen reader says correctly, and an Increase contrast mode

- **Problem.**
  - **Spoken money.** Compact figures are printed as "₦2.8m"
    (`packages/i18n/src/core.ts`, around line 227, lower-cased suffix) and
    used on map pins (`components/app/search/MapCanvas.tsx`, line 739). The
    pin's accessible name is that text plus the title and area, so VoiceOver
    reads it as "naira two point eight m", and some engines say "metres". Price
    periods such as "/yr" and "/mo" (`lib/listings/pricing.ts`, lines 142 to
    152) are read as "slash y r".
  - **Contrast.** `app/css` has no `prefers-contrast` or `forced-colors`
    rule anywhere. The clean direction deliberately uses hairlines, muted inks
    and a dot for the active tab, which is right by default and hard to read
    for low vision or in Lagos midday sun.
- **Proposal.**
  1. Add a `spokenMoney()` beside `formatMoney` ("2.8 million naira a year")
     and a `<Money>` component that prints the compact text with
     `aria-hidden` next to a visually hidden spoken form. Adopt it on pins,
     cards, the sticky price bar and the summary card. Add a test that no
     `compact: true` figure renders without it.
  2. Add a `@media (prefers-contrast: more)` token block: hairlines to the
     secondary border, muted ink to secondary, the active dock tab's label
     underlined as well as coloured, and focus rings 3px. Add a matching
     "Increase contrast" switch in Settings under Appearance, written to the
     root like `data-text-size` (`settings-store.ts`, `applyTextSize`). Add
     `forced-colors: active` fallbacks for the pins, badges and segmented
     thumb, so they keep a visible edge.
- **Impact.** Blind and low-vision users hear real amounts, which matters on a
  platform about the largest sums people spend. Everyone gets a readable app
  outdoors, and it is a store-review and accessibility-audit win.
- **Effort** S for 1. M for 2. **Priority** P2.
- **Founder decision.** None.

### B16. Stories as a sequence: tap through, hold to pause, the next one ready

- **Problem.** A story is a page. `components/social/story/StoryViewer.tsx`
  renders one story with a `more` list of cards under it. There are no tap
  zones, no progress segments, no next-story prefetch and no pause (its only
  timer records the view after 900ms, line 108). Everyone who uses WhatsApp
  Status or Instagram expects tap right for next, tap left for back, and hold
  to pause. Here every next story is a full navigation.
- **Proposal.** Inside the existing viewer:
  - Left and right thirds are tap zones (and the arrow keys). A press and hold
    pauses, and a swipe down closes back to where the story was opened
    (through `chooseBack`, so `BACK_NAVIGATION.md` holds).
  - Thin progress segments sit at the top, one per story in the author's run.
  - The next story's image is prefetched only when not in data saver
    (`lib/ui/lite.ts`) and the connection is not frugal.
  - Auto-advance (six seconds) runs only under Standard and Cinematic motion.
    Calm, Off and reduced motion never advance on a timer. A screen reader hears
    "Story 2 of 5, from {name}" through a polite live region.
- **Impact.** The feed feels native instead of like a set of web pages, and
  data-saver users are never charged for a story they did not open.
- **Effort** M. **Priority** P3.
- **Founder decision.** Whether auto-advance ships at all (default: on under
  Standard, off otherwise).

### B17. Desktop members get a keyboard, and messages get a split view

- **Problem.** At 1440 the member app is a phone layout at reading width.
  Keyboard shortcuts exist only in the admin console (`SupportKeys.tsx`,
  `AdminNav.tsx`). Nothing in `components/app` handles `/`, `j` or `k`. The
  responsive sweep lists "Messages on iPad landscape and desktop is a centred
  single column" as an open item that is "a feature, not a sizing fix".
  Agents and landlords who also rent or book, and diaspora buyers on laptops,
  answer many threads in a row, and each thread is a full page and a back.
- **Proposal.**
  1. **Keyboard layer** (fine pointer only, off in inputs):
     - `/` focuses search.
     - `g h`, `g s`, `g m` and `g p` go to Home, Search, Messages and Plans.
     - `j` and `k` move through inbox rows and search results, and Enter opens
       one.
     - `s` saves the focused listing, and Esc closes sheets.
     - `?` opens a cheat sheet.

     Show each key in the rail's tooltips.
  2. **Messages split** from 1024: the inbox list on the left (360px) and the
     thread on the right, using spec 8.3's panes. The URL still changes per
     thread, so back and deep links hold. Below 1024 nothing changes.
- **Impact.** Heavy users answer three times as many messages in the same
  time. The desktop app reads as a tool (Linear grade), not a stretched phone.
- **Effort** M for 1. L for 2. **Priority** P3.
- **Founder decision.** None.
