# Onboarding a restaurant

**One page for the meeting.** What to collect from a venue owner, in the order
to ask for it, what each thing is for, and what breaks if you leave without it.

Every item below is what the product and the database actually require. Where
the product does not yet do a thing, this page says so plainly rather than
letting you promise it across a table.

---

## Before you open your mouth: three things to be straight about

**1. A table costs nothing.** A guest asks, the venue accepts or declines, and
no money moves at any point. There is no deposit, no booking fee, no cut of the
bill, no card held. Say this in the first minute, because it is the easiest
thing anybody has ever had to agree to. It is also true in the system: the
record of a table carries a party size and a moment, and no amount at all.

**2. We hold nothing and we guarantee nothing.** A booking is a request the
venue answers, in its own time, and it can say no. Do not describe Vallo as
holding a table, holding money, or standing behind a no-show. It does none of
those things.

**3. The verified mark only ever means a human was checked.** It means a person
read a government issued ID and matched it to a real human being. It is not a
food safety rating, not a quality score, and not a statement about the kitchen.
A venue you sign this week gets **no verified mark on its page**, because that
mark is earned through a separate identity review and, on the venue page as it
is built today, a venue on the business side carries no mark at all. Promise
the listing, the hours, the bookings and the enquiries. Do not promise a badge.

---

## The order to ask in

Work down the page. The easy public facts are first because they cost the owner
nothing and get the conversation moving. The bank account is last on purpose:
asking a stranger for account details in the first five minutes ends the
meeting.

Each item is marked:

- **REQUIRED** means the venue cannot take its first table without it.
- **FIRST WEEK** means you can leave the meeting without it and chase it after.

---

### Part one. The venue, as anyone on the street knows it

**1. The name of the place. REQUIRED**
The name that goes on the card, the page and the notification the owner gets
when somebody asks for a table. Without it there is nothing to create.
*One caution:* the name also becomes the venue's web address, so two venues
cannot share a name exactly. If the name is already taken, a small change
(adding the area, for instance) fixes it.

**2. The street address, the city and the state. REQUIRED**
The city and the state are what a guest searches by, and a venue with neither
is unreachable from every shelf in the product. The address is what a guest
navigates to. The state has to be a real Nigerian state; for this week it is
Lagos.

**3. The neighbourhood or area. FIRST WEEK**
Victoria Island, Lekki Phase 1, Yaba. Not strictly required, but the card shows
"area, city" together, so a venue without an area reads as just "Lagos", which
is the difference between a card that looks like a place and a card that looks
like a stub.

**4. A phone number that somebody answers. REQUIRED**
This is how a guest reaches the venue when the app is not enough, and how we
reach the owner. It must be a Nigerian mobile number. Write it however they say
it and the form will tidy it.

**5. A business email address. FIRST WEEK**
Useful, not required. Nothing breaks without it.

**6. Two or three sentences about the place, in the owner's own words. FIRST WEEK**
If you leave without this, the page writes a flat line from the name, the area
and the first cuisine, and it reads like a directory entry rather than a
restaurant. Ask for it while you are sitting there: it takes ninety seconds and
it is the difference between a card somebody taps and a card somebody scrolls
past.

**7. Where the front door is on a map. FIRST WEEK, and see the note**
Worth writing down (a dropped pin over WhatsApp is enough). Be aware that the
application form does not currently ask a restaurant for its map position, so
there is nowhere for the owner to enter it themselves; it has to be put in by
hand afterwards. Without it the venue still appears in search by city and by
area, but it never sorts by distance and never draws on a map. Collect it, pass
it to whoever does the data entry.

---

### Part two. What they serve, and when. This is the part that matters

**8. What kind of food it is. FIRST WEEK**
A short list, for example Nigerian, Grill, Seafood. Up to twelve. The first one
shows on the venue's fact strip; the rest become tags. A venue with none shows
an empty strip, which reads as a record nobody finished.

**9. A price band, one to four. REQUIRED**
Cheapest to dearest. Not an amount, and never an amount: we do not publish a
price per head that the venue would then have to honour. The application cannot
be sent without this.

**10. THE HOURS AND THE SEATS. REQUIRED, AND THIS IS THE ONE PEOPLE SKIP**

For each day the venue serves, you need four numbers:

- when the kitchen **opens**,
- the **last seating**, the latest time they will start a new table,
- when it **closes**,
- and **how many guests they will take across that whole service**.

A venue with lunch and dinner gives you two sets for the same day.

**Why this is the one that matters.** Vallo works out whether a venue can take a
booking entirely from these rows. If a venue has none, every single request is
refused by the system with the words "that restaurant does not seat guests at
that time", and the owner will sit there for a fortnight wondering why nobody is
booking while their page looks perfectly fine. Their card also reads "Hours not
published", which is exactly what it sounds like to a hungry person choosing
between two restaurants.

**Four things to get right while you have them in the room:**

- **The guest number is not the seat count.** Vallo adds up every guest already
  booked into that service and refuses the next request once the total would go
  over. It does not know about table turnover, and it does not know about walk
  ins or people who ring the venue directly. So the number to write down is *how
  many guests they are happy to receive through Vallo in that service*, which for
  most venues is a fraction of the dining room, not all of it. Starting small and
  raising it later is the right advice.
- **The last seating is usually not the closing time.** A kitchen that closes at
  22:00 does not want a party of six walking in at 21:58. Ask for it separately
  and they will thank you.
- **A service has to end on the same day it starts.** A venue serving until 2am
  writes the evening service as ending at 23:59 and takes its last seating before
  that. It is not a problem, but you need to know it so you are not confused at
  the table.
- **Guests book on Lagos time.** Whatever a guest's phone thinks, 19:30 means
  19:30 in the dining room.

**11. The menu link, the dress code, and three yes-or-no questions. FIRST WEEK**
The three questions are: is there parking, is there power backup, is there
outdoor seating. They sound minor and they are not: they are the three things a
Lagos diner checks before choosing, and parking and power backup show on the
venue's card in search, so a venue that answers them is more findable than one
that does not. A menu link has to be a secure (https) web address.

---

### Part three. The person behind it

You are now asking about a human rather than a business, so the tone changes.
Explain what it is for: we check that a real, contactable person stands behind
every first-party venue, and that check is the only thing the verified mark ever
means.

**12. The representative's full name and phone number. REQUIRED**
The person who signs for the venue and who will answer the account. Their phone
must be a Nigerian mobile number.

**13. One government issued ID for that person. REQUIRED**
An international passport, a driver's licence, a NIN slip or card, or a
permanent voter's card. A clear photograph or a PDF, all four corners in frame,
the photograph and the numbers readable, under 8MB. Without it the application
cannot be sent at all.
*Never write an ID number down anywhere outside the upload. The file goes into
the application and nowhere else.*

---

### Part four. The paperwork

**14. The hygiene and food safety attestation. REQUIRED**

This is a tick in the application, and the exact words the representative is
agreeing to are:

> This venue holds a current Local Government health permit and its food
> handlers hold current medical certificates.

**Who signs it:** the representative, from item 12, in their own account. Not
you, not the chef, not the manager unless the manager is the representative on
the application. It is their statement, dated the moment they tick it, and it
shows on the record as a dated fact with their name against it. It is not a
badge, it does not light anything up on the venue's page, and nobody should be
told it does.

The application cannot be sent without it. If the owner will not tick it, that
is the meeting telling you something, and the right answer is to stop and ask
why rather than to work around it.

**15. The health permit itself. FIRST WEEK**
The current Local Government health permit as a photograph or PDF. Optional
alongside the attestation, and worth collecting because it is the evidence
behind a statement somebody has already made.

**16. The state hospitality licence. FIRST WEEK**
The current licence or registration from the state tourism or hospitality body,
if they hold one. Optional. Like the health permit, it records as a dated fact,
never as a badge.

**17. The CAC registration question. FIRST WEEK, and read this carefully**

Ask: "is the restaurant registered with the CAC, and do you have the
certificate?" Then one of two things happens.

**If they are registered,** collect three things: the name exactly as the CAC
holds it (often not the name on the sign), the RC or BN number from the
certificate, and a photograph of the certificate. The number is checked by a
person against the CAC public search later. Their TIN if they have it, but it is
not required.

**If they are not registered, sign them anyway.** This is the important part and
it is not a workaround: a restaurant is not required to be CAC registered to go
on Vallo or to take its first table. The registration paperwork is only required
of an operator who came in as a registered hospitality business, which a
restaurant does not. A great many real Lagos restaurants trade on a business
name registration, or on nothing at all, and refusing them would be refusing the
market.

What they give up by not being registered is height on the verification ladder,
nothing else. The ladder is identity, then registration, then payout, then a
visit, and it only counts rungs with no gap below them, so a venue with no
registration stops at the first rung whatever else it passes. Tell them that
plainly: "you can go live today, and registering later moves you up."

Two useful notes: a **BN** (business name) number counts just as an RC does, and
many owners have one without thinking of themselves as registered, so ask the
question twice in different words. And never go digging on the CAC portal
yourself; the number gets checked properly by the person who reviews it.

**18. Proof that the representative acts for the business. FIRST WEEK**
Only relevant when the person signing is not named on the CAC record. A letter
of authorisation on the business's letterhead, or an employment ID, naming both
them and the business, and signed.

### Part five. The bank account, which is last for a reason

**19. Bank and account number for payouts. REQUIRED to send the application**

**Say what it is for before you ask for it, because the obvious guess is wrong.**
It is not for table money: there is none, and there never will be on a
reservation. It is the account any money Vallo ever owes the venue would be paid
into, and it is a question the application asks of every operator before it can
be sent, restaurant or hotel. Nothing about a table touches it.

**How it works in the room:** you need the bank and the ten digit account
number, and nothing else. We ask the bank whose account it is, show the owner
the name the bank gave back, and they confirm it. The name we store is always
the bank's answer, never anything typed into a form. This is exactly what every
Nigerian banking app does before a transfer, and owners recognise it
immediately.

**The name-matching problem, which will come up.** The bank will very often
return a personal name when the restaurant trades under a different one, or a
registered name that does not match the sign, or a spelling of a Nigerian name
that differs by a character. **This does not block anything.** The account still
saves, the application still sends, and the venue can still go live and take
tables. What it does is leave the payout rung of the verification ladder open
for a person to look at instead of passing automatically. So the honest sentence
is: "if the account is in your own name and the restaurant is not, that is fine,
it just means somebody here looks at it rather than a computer waving it
through."

If the owner is not comfortable giving an account in the meeting, take
everything else, and tell them the application waits for that one field. It is a
five minute job afterwards over the phone.

---

### Part six. The three permissions

**20. Three separate ticks. REQUIRED**

Three, not one, and they are deliberately not bundled:

1. the documents uploaded are genuine and the details are accurate,
2. acceptance of the host terms and the privacy policy,
3. permission to process their details for identity and fraud checks.

The third one is a Nigerian data protection requirement and it has to be its own
freely given decision, which is why it is not folded into the other two. Each
tick is stored with the moment it was made.

---

### Part seven. Photographs

**21. Photographs of the venue. FIRST WEEK, and be honest about where they go**

Collect them to the standard the rest of the product already enforces on a
property: four to ten, at least 1600 pixels across, under 50MB each, JPEG, PNG,
WEBP or HEIC (an iPhone straight out of the camera roll is fine). A phone
camera in good light beats a bad professional shoot; the owner's own recent
photographs are usually better than anything on their Instagram from 2021. Shoot
the room as a guest first sees it, a table laid, the frontage so somebody can
recognise it from the street, and two or three plates they are actually known
for. Whoever takes them, agree out loud that Vallo may use them on the venue's
page.

**What you must not promise.** The venue record on the restaurant side does not
carry photographs today. A venue's page currently shows a Vallo category
photograph with a "No photographs yet" label over it, so a guest is never misled
into thinking a stock picture is that dining room, but the owner's own
photographs will not appear on their page the day they sign. Collect them
anyway, hand them to whoever does the data entry, and tell the owner they go up
when the venue page can carry them. Do not say they will be live tomorrow.

---

## What he can leave the meeting without

Everything marked FIRST WEEK. In one line: **name, address, city, state, a phone
number, a price band, at least one service window with hours and a guest number,
the representative's name, phone and ID, the hygiene tick, a bank account and
the three permissions.** That is the whole of it. Cuisines, photographs, the
description, the menu link, the CAC papers, the health permit and the map pin
can all follow.

---

## What happens next

Tell the owner this, in this order, so the following week is not a silence.

1. **The application is sent.** Nothing goes anywhere until they press the button
   themselves, and the form tells them exactly what is still missing if anything
   is.
2. **A person reads it.** Not a script. They get a notification either way. If
   something needs changing, the note comes back in the reviewer's own words and
   they can answer it and send again.
3. **The venue is put on the shelf.** It appears in search for its city and area,
   with its hours, its open-or-closed line, and its parking and power backup if
   they answered those.
4. **The identity check.** A person reads the ID against the representative. This
   is the only thing that earns a verified mark, and it is about the human, not
   the restaurant.
5. **The first table.** A guest asks, the owner gets a notification naming the
   guest, the party and the time on the Lagos clock, and accepts or declines. The
   guest is told either way. A thread opens beside the booking so the two can
   talk about a late arrival or a high chair.

**Two things to be careful about when you set expectations for the week.**

There is no self-service button that puts a restaurant on the shelf today: step
three is done by hand by the team, not by the owner and not by a console screen.
And a restaurant owner has no page of their own yet for accepting tables, so
until that exists the venue needs to be set up so the owner can reach the
booking queue. Agree the timing with whoever is doing the data entry before you
promise the owner a date. Do not tell an owner they will be taking bookings
tomorrow unless somebody has confirmed both of those are handled.

**The things not to say, ever:** that the venue is verified before the identity
check has been done; that Vallo holds money, a deposit or a guarantee on a
table; that a table costs the guest or the venue anything; or that photographs
they hand over will be on their page immediately.

## For whoever does the data entry

**This section names tables and columns. Its reader is an engineer.** Everything
below was read out of the migrations and confirmed against the live schema
before it was written. Nothing here has been applied to any database.

### Where each answer goes

| # | Asked for | Lands in |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Venue name | `businesses.name`, and `businesses.slug` (unique, lower-case, hyphenated) |
| 2 | Street address, city, state | `businesses.address`, `businesses.city`, `businesses.state_code` (FK to `states.code`; Lagos is `LA`) |
| 3 | Area | `businesses.area` |
| 4 | Phone | `businesses.phone`, constrained to `^\+234[7-9][0-9]{9}$` |
| 5 | Email | `businesses.email` |
| 6 | Description | `businesses.description`, up to 4000 characters |
| 7 | Map position | `businesses.latitude` / `businesses.longitude`; `businesses.location` is derived by the `businesses_location_sync` trigger and is never written directly |
| 8 | Cuisines | `restaurant_profiles.cuisines`, `text[]`, at most 12 |
| 9 | Price band | `restaurant_profiles.price_band`, smallint 1 to 4 |
| 10 | Hours and guest number | one `service_windows` row per service: `weekday` (0 is Sunday, `extract(dow)`), `opens`, `last_seating`, `closes`, `covers` (> 0, <= 2000). Unique on `(business_id, weekday, opens)`; `opens < closes` and `opens <= last_seating <= closes` |
| 11 | Menu, dress code, facilities | `restaurant_profiles.menu_url` (must start `https://`), `.dress_code`, `.parking`, `.power_backup`, `.outdoor` |
| 12 | Representative name and phone | `businesses.representative_name`, `businesses.representative_phone` |
| 13 | ID document | file into the private `host-documents` bucket under `<auth uid>/...`, plus a `business_documents` row with `kind = 'identity'` |
| 14 | Hygiene attestation | `businesses.hygiene_attested_at` |
| 15 | Health permit file | `business_documents` with `kind = 'hygiene'` |
| 16 | State licence | `businesses.licence_attested_at`, and `business_documents` with `kind = 'licence'` |
| 17 | CAC | `businesses.registered_name`, `businesses.cac_number` (loose check: `^(RC\|BN\|IT)?\s?-?\d{4,10}$`), `businesses.tin`, and `business_documents` with `kind = 'registration'` |
| 18 | Authority to act | `business_documents` with `kind = 'association'` |
| 19 | Bank account | `bank_accounts` (user-scoped by `user_id`, not business-scoped). `resolved_account_name` is NOT NULL and must be Paystack's answer, never typed |
| 20 | The three consents | `businesses.consents` jsonb, `{"accuracy": "<iso>", "terms": "<iso>", "processing": "<iso>"}`. An absent key is a consent not given |
| 21 | Photographs | **nowhere today.** There is no photo table on the business spine. `listing_photos` belongs to `listings`, `accommodation_photos` to `accommodations`, and a restaurant business has neither |

Also set at creation, by the application or by hand:
`businesses.kind = 'restaurant'`, `businesses.source = 'first_party'`,
`businesses.owner_id` = the owner's `auth.users` id, `businesses.is_demo = false`,
`businesses.host_type = 'restaurant'`.

### The gate on a table, from `private.reservation_is_valid`

A `reservations` row with `business_id` set (and `listing_id` null, which
`reservations_exactly_one_target_chk` enforces) is refused unless **all** of:

- the business exists and `kind = 'restaurant'`;
- `status = 'PUBLISHED'`;
- `source = 'first_party'`;
- `is_demo` is false;
- a `service_windows` row exists for the Lagos weekday of `reserved_for` with
  `opens <= local time <= last_seating`, else "that restaurant does not seat
  guests at that time";
- the sum of `party_size` over `PENDING` and `CONFIRMED` rows for that business
  on that Lagos date, between the chosen window's `opens` and `closes`, plus this
  party, is `<= covers`;
- and `reserved_for > now()`.

Note the capacity sum is per window per Lagos date, so `covers` is the total
guests a service will accept through Vallo, not a seat count and not a per-sitting
figure. Walk-ins and telephone bookings are invisible to it.

`public.refuse_transaction_on_demo_listing` sits on `reservations` as
`reservations_never_against_a_demo_listing` and reads `listing_id` only; the
example refusal on the business path is the `is_demo` branch inside
`reservation_is_valid` above. Both were confirmed live against `pg_proc` and
`pg_trigger`, byte for byte with the migrations.

### Things the reader should know before promising a date

- **`status = 'PUBLISHED'` with `published_at` set is the gate, and nothing in
  the product sets it for a restaurant.** `approveBusiness` in
  `apps/web/src/lib/admin/business-actions.ts` moves a business to `APPROVED`
  only. The single code path that writes `PUBLISHED` on `businesses` is
  `publishAccommodation`, which requires an `accommodations` row, a pin, a photo
  and a room type with a rate. A restaurant has no accommodation, so it never
  reaches that path. There is also no `admin/businesses` route: the review and
  rung actions exist and nothing imports them.
- **The owner has no surface for accepting tables.** `private.notify_reservation`
  points a business host at `/host/reservations`, which does not exist. The
  reservations board lives at `/agent/bookings` and is guarded by
  `getAgentContext`, which requires an `agents` row keyed to the user. A host
  onboarded through the restaurant branch has a `businesses` row and no `agents`
  row.
- **The guest must not be the owner.** Nothing in the schema forbids it, but
  `getHostReservations` filters with `.neq("guest_id", <self>)`, so a table the
  owner books at their own venue never appears in their own queue.
- **The badge has two different derivations and neither lights up here.**
  `businesses.verified` is derived by trigger from `business_verification_checks`
  (identity rung passed, first-party only). The catalogue projection's `verified`
  is derived separately, from `agent_badges.verified` through
  `businesses.agent_id`. The restaurant detail page hard-codes `verified: false`
  for a venue on the business spine, with the comment that `source` is not what
  the mark records. So a restaurant business shows no verified mark on its page
  today under any combination of rungs.
- **Rungs are recorded by hand.** `recordBusinessRung` is admin only and writes
  `business_verification_checks`; the tier and the badge are recomputed by
  trigger and are never written directly. No automated payout check exists on the
  business spine. The automated bank-name match
  (`private.verify_payout_account`, leaving the rung `pending` on a mismatch with
  both names in the note) runs on the **agent** ladder against `payout_accounts`,
  not on `bank_accounts` and not on a business.
- **A venue with no pin is still searchable.** `search_catalogue` admits a row
  with a null `location` inside a radius filter; it simply never ranks by
  distance. So a missing pin costs distance sorting and the map, not visibility.
