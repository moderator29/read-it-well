# The Hundred

**What would make Vallo excellent, and what would make it uncatchable.**

Written 23 September 2026 for the founder of VALLO SPACES LTD, against `main` at `85c5471` and the live product at www.vallospaces.com. It sits beside the parallel audit session: that session finds what is broken, and this file proposes what should exist. Nothing here is a bug report. Where the walk tripped over something broken, it went to the audit session, and the list is in TO THE FOUNDER.

Three sections open the file, as asked: the ten to do first, the single one, and what would make Vallo uncatchable. Every recommendation carries an ID from V-01 to V-100, and **the ID is the rank**. How the file was made, what the walk found, the field, and a ranked index of all hundred follow, then the hundred in full, grouped by area, then what we rejected and everything addressed to the founder.

## THE TEN YOU WOULD DO FIRST

Not the top ten of the ranking, although most of them are there. These are the ten that, done in this order, stop harm, get the app through review, make trust visible, and start the supply loop.

1. **V-33 Rent never rests at Vallo.** Its first step takes an hour: delete "the agent has been paid" from the three success screens today. Then settle rent to the lister at the moment of charge. Today the live path holds a tenant's whole move-in total with no payout, which is the custody question the escrow ADR refuses, arrived at by the back door.
2. **V-01 The catalogue canary.** The eleven-and-a-half-hour outage was found by the founder. Nothing else in this file matters if nobody knows the shop is shut.
3. **V-02 The claims rule becomes a build check.** Delete the seven false sentences found on the walk this week, then make the rule executable so the eighth cannot ship.
4. **V-11 The app never opens on the website**, with V-52 beside it. A reviewer's first second in the binary is currently a marketing page with a footer, which is the literal description of a 4.2 rejection.
5. **V-03 The proof strip.** Show the dated facts the database already holds, on the card and the page, and nothing that is null.
6. **V-04 The account-number moment.** The person receiving an account number is told whether it belongs to the verified lister, and offered the real charge instead.
7. **V-31 The owner confirms vacancy.** The first half of the single one, below. Start collecting consent at every mandate call now, even before the channel is live.
8. **V-07 The share door**, then V-08 the code on the gate. Under the sign-in ruling, Vallo is currently invisible on WhatsApp, which is where Nigerian property is discussed.
9. **V-06 No paid placement, ever.** Delete the `featured` column nothing sets and publish the ranking in words. It is a position no incumbent can take without abolishing its revenue.
10. **V-09 Paste your broadcast.** The agent pastes the WhatsApp message they already wrote and it becomes a draft. It is the cheapest possible answer to the only problem the platform has: there is no real supply.

## THE SINGLE ONE YOU WOULD DO IF YOU COULD ONLY DO ONE

### Bring the landlord into the room

**Give the landlord one line into Vallo, on the number already stored in `listing_mandates.principal_phone`, with their consent recorded at the mandate call. Use it for two things: every fortnight they confirm the flat is still free (V-31), and the day a tenant pays they are shown, to the kobo, what was paid and asked to confirm it (V-32).**

Every Nigerian who has ever hunted for a flat has thought the same sentence: *if only I could ask the landlord.* Every problem in the brief runs through the fact that nobody can.

- **The flat let three months ago is still advertised** because every platform asks the agent whether it is available, and the agent is the one person in the chain who profits from the answer being yes. The landlord always knows whether the flat is let and gains nothing from a stale advert. Ask them, and the incentive problem disappears.
- **The same flat posted by four agents** is four listings naming one principal. Key the property on that principal, hashed and never shown, and the four become one page with four offers side by side (V-37). When the landlord answers "let", all four come down in the same minute (V-48).
- **The agent who is not the agent** cannot produce a principal who confirms them. The landlord who answers "I never instructed this agent" catches the fraud before a renter pays.
- **The rent markup** (the landlord told ₦2.5m, the tenant charged ₦2.8m, the agent keeping the difference) is exposed on the day of payment, to the one person who can end the mandate.
- **The deposit that never comes back** now has a named debtor who acknowledged, in writing, receiving it. That is the foundation the caution register (V-36) needs.
- **The ugliest Lagos story**, where the tenant paid, the agent kept it, and the landlord evicts, ends with the tenant holding a dated confirmation from the landlord himself.

**Why nobody has done it.** Every Nigerian property product is built on one of two assumptions. Either the agent is the customer (the classifieds, which are paid by agents and cannot hand an agent's landlord a direct line to the platform), or the platform replaces the agent (Spleet, Rentsmallsmall, Muster, which become the landlord's agent and so own the conflict). Both put the landlord out of sight of the transaction. Vallo charges agents nothing for placement and publishes the principal's number to nobody, so it can make the landlord a participant without making him a lead.

**Why it is obvious in hindsight.** Vallo already has both halves. The mandate migration stores the principal's name and number because, in its own words, they are "what make it checkable", and the call-back is "the check". Then the platform never speaks to them again. `rent_payments` already freezes the six parts to the kobo. The join is one table, one consent sentence, one message a fortnight and one tokenised page in Vallo's own chrome, showing the area and never the address. The landlord needs no account and no app; he answers 1, 2 or 3.

**Why it beats the other two candidates.** The four agents each nominated a best idea, and two of the others are superb: the flat that remembers its last let and seeds its own successor listing (V-38), and the code on the gate that replaces the phone number on the TO LET board (V-08). Both are in this file and both matter. But both need supply to exist before they do anything. The landlord's line works on the first mandate Vallo approves, it is what makes the other two honest (a successor listing and a gate code are only as true as the availability behind them), and it attacks the single loudest complaint in the market at its cause rather than at its symptom.

**What would make it fail, said plainly.** Landlords who do not answer: so silence is not a negative, and only after 21 days does a listing sort last as "Not reconfirmed". Landlords abroad: WhatsApp reaches them better than SMS, and the message works in both. A "principal" number that is really the agent's second SIM: the mandate call-back must catch it, and the phone and collusion checks (V-50, V-58) make it visible. Agents who resist and stop uploading mandates: the mandate stays a dated fact rather than a requirement, and "Owner confirmed available 3 days ago" beside "No owner on record" will do the persuading. Cost: at ₦5 to ₦7 a message it is under two naira a day per listing. The consent sentence is not optional; messaging a non-user without it is not lawful under the NDPA.

## WHAT WOULD MAKE VALLO UNCATCHABLE

A feature can be copied by Friday. A position cannot, when holding it requires a competitor to become four things it is not, all at once, and then wait a year for data it can never backfill. These four, taken together, are that position.

**1. The landlord in the room.** V-31, V-32, V-37, V-48. Property truth sourced from the only disinterested party in the chain, one page per physical flat, and "let" as an event that takes every copy down. A classifieds site cannot do it without betraying the agents who pay it; a managed-rent company cannot do it because it is the landlord's agent.

**2. The gate.** V-35, V-05, V-34. The inspection is the one moment a real person stands in front of the real property, and every competitor throws that knowledge away. Proving at the gate that the agent is the agent, asking the renter four truth questions that evening, and counting both into a Record that the agent carries off the platform turns reputation from a claim into an attendance register. Its inputs are events a portal never sees.

**3. The money after the gate.** V-36, V-47, V-39, V-38. A caution recorded as owed with a due-back date and a public return rate; a tenancy file that freezes what was promised; asking prices joined by what people actually paid; and a flat that remembers its last let. Nigeria has no sold-price feed and over 90 per cent of land is unregistered. Every tenancy that moves through Vallo writes a row no competitor will ever hold, and the rows compound.

**4. The promise nobody else can make.** V-06 and V-12. No paid placement, ever, and every fee printed as a share of the rent beside the law's number. These are not features; they are a business model. PropertyPro sells push-ups every three days and "sponsored" at twenty times exposure. For an incumbent to copy these two lines it would have to give up the revenue that pays for it.

**Why together.** Each one leans on the others. The Record is only credible because inspections are proved at the gate. The paid-price data is only honest because the landlord confirmed the figure. The ranking can refuse money because the Record gives it something better to rank on. A competitor who wants to follow must become a verification operation, a transaction rail, a native app on both sides of a gate, and a business that does not sell visibility, and then start every record at zero on the day it begins. Vallo's head start is not the code. It is the year of attended inspections, confirmed vacancies and paid tenancies that nobody else will be collecting.

## How this file was made, and how to read it

**Four agents, one register.** Discovery and supply; demand, money and the transaction; identity, trust, safety and communication; the platform, the craft and the experience as a whole. Each read the repository and the research that rests on it (`docs/PRODUCT.md`, the three HANDOFF briefs, the escrow, valuation and role-architecture research, `RECOMMENDATIONS.md` so nothing already queued was proposed twice), then **walked the live product before writing a word**: signed out, as the QA member and as the QA admin, at 390 by 844, including a slow-3G profile with a 4x CPU slowdown, offline, and as a WhatsApp link unfurler. Nothing was paid, booked, listed or published. The four kept one shared register so no idea was claimed twice. They proposed 120; this file keeps 100. Nineteen were merged into a sharper neighbour, and one small privacy fix went to the audit session.

**Every recommendation fits the system that exists.** Each one names the component, table or route it extends, and each respects the rules the product already lives by: two sides, personal and working mode, three supply roles at the listing level; dark only, one blue family with emerald, rose and cyan, the token layer, the shared panel, card, plate, sheet and drawer, 390px first; the shape law; the claims rule; integer kobo in one append-only ledger with no balance columns; and nobody leaves Vallo. Where a recommendation needs something new, it says which pattern in the codebase it copies. Nothing here reopens peer-to-peer sends to external bank accounts or relitigates the Paystack upgrade.

**The shape of each entry.** `ID | AREA | IMPACT | EFFORT | DIFFERENTIATOR? | TITLE`, then WHAT, WHY, FITS BY, WHERE, HOW, WHAT IT BEATS, WHO ELSE HAS IT, CAN IT BE COPIED. A line under the heading marks the entries that delete something and the quiet wins.

**The ID is the rank.** V-01 is the first thing to do and V-100 the last. The ranking is impact over effort with the weights written down so they can be argued with: TRANSFORMATIVE counts 6, HIGH 3, MEDIUM 2, POLISH 1; hours divide by 1, days by 2, weeks by 4. The weights are deliberate. With a flat scale, every transformative piece of work that takes weeks would sink beneath every tidy-up that takes an hour, and the file would tell you to polish before you build. Within a score, what other entries depend on goes first. The body is grouped by area so related work reads together; inside each area the entries stay in rank order, and the index below lists all hundred in one ranked table.

**Old identifiers.** References such as `MK-63`, `G-1`, `E-1` or three-digit IDs like `A1-014` point at rows in `RECOMMENDATIONS.md`. References of the form `V-nn` point inside this file.

**The count.** One hundred recommendations. **49 are marked DIFFERENTIATOR**: things no Nigerian competitor has and none can get quickly, each with the reason in CAN IT BE COPIED. **13 recommend deleting something**, and **17 are quiet wins**: small, cheap, invisible until missing.

## What the walk said, in one page

Four agents walked the same product on the same evening and came back with the same six sentences.

1. **The trust machinery is designed and the trust experience is not.** `mandate_verified_at`, `ownership_verified_at`, a mandate table holding the landlord's own name and number, an eight-item inspection report, a canonical-mailbox recorder, a device recorder: almost none of it reaches a renter's eye, and the anon role is refused the dates. Meanwhile `/rent` says "Every rental here is checked" above cards that each say nothing was verified.
2. **Money is superb up to the gate and silent after it.** The move-in breakdown is the most honest money screen in Nigerian property. After the tenant pays ₦4.2m there is no tenancy they can open in six months, no record that anybody owes the caution back, no document the landlord has seen, no renewal date. And the live rent path settles the whole move-in total to nobody while the success screen says "the agent has been paid". That one is urgent and is V-33.
3. **Since 23 September the platform is behind a sign-in wall, and nothing downstream was redesigned for it.** A listing pasted into WhatsApp unfurls as "Sign in | Vallo". A mistyped URL becomes a four-slide carousel. The one per-page share image the product built can never be fetched by a crawler. None of the recommendations here reopens the catalogue; three of them (V-07, V-08, V-82) make Vallo travel anyway.
4. **On a Nigerian connection the product takes 8 to 11 seconds to paint anything**, and the service worker built to fix that has never cached a single chunk in production. Warm from cache, `/search` falls from 1.5 MB and 17.9 seconds to 128 KB and 4.4.
5. **The supply tools are honest and empty.** Analytics says on screen that views are not counted. The inbox is a list. Three supply doors, and none says what a lister gets that WhatsApp does not.
6. **The craft is real.** The empty states are the best-written screens in any Nigerian product; the console's 27 desks each say what they are for; reconciliation reads "Clean in 55 of 55 runs". This is a platform worth making excellent, which is why the bar in this file is high.

**The regulatory weather.** The Lagos Tenancy and Recovery of Premises Bill is still a bill in September 2026. It proposes 5 per cent agency commission, no more than a year upfront for new tenants, refundable deposits less documented damage, and mandatory LASRERA registration. The 2011 law (10 per cent agency, 10 per cent legal, one year upfront, with exemptions for Ikoyi, Victoria Island, Ikeja GRA and Apapa) remains in force. Every competitor will print "5% cap!" the week it is signed. Vallo is the only one that can show, per listing and per transaction, whether it is being obeyed.

**What none of them has, said once.** A transaction, so no price actually paid. An inspection record, so no attendance. A verified identity graph, so no record that follows a person. Both sides on one platform, so a let never becomes the next listing. And a business model that does not sell placement. Those five are the whole of Vallo's room to move.

### V-96 | TRUST | HIGH | weeks | not a differentiator | WhatsApp is a doorbell, never a room; one channel policy for metered data

## Money and the transaction

The engineering under money is the best in any Nigerian product: integer kobo, one ledger, invariants that catch real errors. The experience of money stops at the moment of payment. After the gate is where Nigerian tenants lose money, so after the gate is where Vallo has to be.

23 recommendations here, in rank order.

### V-04 | MONEY | TRANSFORMATIVE | days | DIFFERENTIATOR | The account-number moment: when money talk starts in a thread, the receiver is checked and the real charge appears

**WHAT** When a message carrying a ten-digit NUBAN or payment language arrives in a thread, the person receiving it sees one card above the digits, identical in shape on both sides. It does two things. First, the check: Vallo resolves the account (NUBAN check digits narrow 25 banks to one or two) and compares the holder with the name Vallo verified for this listing's lister, then says only "This account does not belong to the person Vallo verified for this listing" or "This account belongs to the verified lister. Vallo still cannot protect a payment made to an account." The resolved name is never shown. Second, the alternative: if the thread has an accepted inspection, the card offers the real thing, "Paying for this place? Pay the move-in total here: ₦4,200,000, the figure on the listing, recorded to the kobo", with a Pay button to `/rent/pay/[inspectionId]`; with no accepted inspection it says "Pay only after inspection. Request one here." When held payments open, the agency-fee version appears. Report and Block sit on the same card.

**WHY** In this market the account number travels from the agent to the renter, and the moment it is typed is the moment the platform loses the transaction. Today the education card in `lib/messages/education.ts` warns the person typing, and `private.scan_message` files a flag to an admin queue the renter never sees. The renter about to transfer ₦1.8m sees the digits and nothing else. This turns the scammer's own tool into evidence at the one second it matters, and offers the built rent charge exactly when temptation peaks.

**FITS BY** `private.scan_message` and `message_flags` (`20260728222112_messaging_trust.sql`), `sendMessage` in `lib/messages/actions.ts`, account resolution in `lib/payments/bank-resolve.ts`, the b4 rule that a flag never keeps the account number (`20260919101700_b4_a_flag_stops_keeping_the_account_number.sql`), `inspection_requests` CONFIRMED, the thread's system-card pattern, `ReportSheet` and `blocks`.

**WHERE** `apps/web/src/lib/messages/actions.ts`, `lib/messages/education.ts`, `lib/payments/bank-resolve.ts`, `app/(app)/messages/[id]/page.tsx` and the thread renderer, new `public.message_account_checks (message_id pk, bank_code, last4, name_matches_lister boolean, checked_at)`, a system message kind `charge_offer`.

**HOW** The trigger cannot call Paystack, so the check runs in the send action after insert, asynchronously; the receiver's view shows a neutral "checking" state for the second it takes. Store the match boolean and last four only. Rate-limit resolutions per conversation through `private.consume_rate_limit` so the feature cannot become a free name-lookup oracle, and never run it for the sender's own view. The charge offer is idempotent per thread per day and never blocks the message.

**WHAT IT BEATS** Blocking messages with digits (scammers spell them out within a day, and phone numbers trip it); showing the resolved name (Vallo becomes a lookup service for strangers' account names, an NDPA problem); a nag banner (ignored).

**WHO ELSE HAS IT** Nobody. Banks show the name to the payer at transfer time, which is exactly too late. Jiji shows safety tips. Nobody replaces the transfer with a charge.

**CAN IT BE COPIED** It needs in-platform messaging, a verified lister name, a resolver and a frozen charge in the same product. Portals have none of the first two and no charge. A year.

### V-12 | MONEY | HIGH | hours | DIFFERENTIATOR | Print each fee as a share of the rent, beside the law's number

*QUIET WIN*

**WHAT** Beside every agency, legal and agreement fee, print its share of the annual rent: "Agency fee ₦280,000 · 10.0% of a year's rent". Beneath the three, print one line: "Fees to the agent: ₦700,000, 25.0% of a year's rent." **WHY** The breakdown I walked is honest and still made me do arithmetic to discover that 25 per cent of the rent goes to the agent. The "agent-fee racket" is invisible because nobody expresses it as the unit people compare. Nairametrics (Oct 2025) reports Lagos tenants paying roughly double on fees against the 10 per cent rule. A ratio is integer arithmetic in basis points, costs hours, and turns every listing into evidence. When the draft 5 per cent bill is signed, Vallo changes one constant and every listing is instantly measured against it.

**FITS BY** `lib/listings/pricing.ts` (`moveInParts`) computes the basis points as integers, following PRODUCT.md's "percentages are integer basis points". `ListingMoveIn.tsx`, `rent/move-in/[listingId]`, and the search sort alongside the existing move-in sort that reads `total_move_in_cost_minor`. **WHERE** `lib/listings/pricing.ts`, `components/app/listing/ListingMoveIn.tsx`, `app/(app)/rent/move-in/[listingId]/page.tsx`, the search sort list, and a new `lib/trust/fee-rules.ts` with a state code, rule basis points, source and date.

**HOW** `bps = (fee_minor * 10000) / rent_annualised_minor`, using integer division and displayed to one decimal via `formatPercent`. Annualise quarter and month by multiplication only. Show the state line only where `state_code = 'LA'`. Never colour a ratio red: it is a fact, and colour alone is not a signal.

**WHAT IT BEATS** Capping fees (the founder has already refused, and it would drive agents off). A "fair fee" badge (a verdict the platform then has to defend).

**WHO ELSE HAS IT** Nobody, because every classified is paid by the agents it would expose.

**CAN IT BE COPIED** Technically in a day. Commercially, never by an agent-subscription classified. Only Vallo also knows whether the fee was **actually** charged (V-39).

### V-13 | MONEY | HIGH | hours | not a differentiator | The quote is binding: frozen when the lister accepts the inspection, with nothing left unexplained

*QUIET WIN*

**WHAT** Two fixes to the move-in figure. (1) Freeze the six parts on the inspection the moment the lister moves it to CONFIRMED, not at the tenant's first tap on Pay. ### V-32 | MONEY | TRANSFORMATIVE | weeks | DIFFERENTIATOR | The landlord countersigns the rent

**WHAT** When a tenant pays a move-in charge on a listing that carries an approved letting mandate, the principal named on that mandate (`listing_mandates.principal_name`, reached on `principal_phone`) receives an SMS and, where an email is on file, an email, holding a one-time link to a Vallo page. The page shows, in the landlord's words:

- "A tenant has paid ₦2,800,000 rent for one year, from 1 November 2026, for your property in Ikeja GRA, through [agent name] on Vallo."
- The six parts as frozen on `rent_payments`, including the ₦560,000 caution the tenant has paid "for you".
- Two buttons: "That is right" and "That is not what I agreed".

The tenant's tenancy page then shows one of three dated facts: "The landlord, [first name], confirmed these figures on 2 Nov", "The landlord has not answered yet", or "The landlord disputes these figures". The landlord never needs an account. The page lives inside Vallo's chrome at a tokenised route and shows no street address, only the area.

**WHY** The two most expensive rental frauds in Lagos are the agent who is not the landlord's agent, and the agent who **marks up the rent**: telling the landlord ₦2.5m, charging the tenant ₦2.8m and pocketing the difference. A caution deposit the landlord says they never received is the same fraud a year later. Nothing on any Nigerian platform lets the person who owns the flat see the number the tenant paid. Vallo already has the principal's name and phone as a column precisely so a mandate is "checkable" (migration `20260922230400_track_g_5` header), and it already freezes the exact kobo figures on `rent_payments`. Joining those two facts is the whole feature. It turns the mandate from a document an admin once looked at into a live, two-sided attestation on every tenancy. It also gives V-36 (the caution register) a named, acknowledging party to owe the deposit back.

**FITS BY** `listing_mandates` (principal_name and principal_phone, kept private exactly as that migration requires: the tenant sees only the landlord's first name and the dated answer, never the phone). `rent_payments` is the frozen figure. The acknowledgement page follows the tokenised-route pattern of the price-check share (`price_check_shares`), with no address, per the standing rule. Notifications go through `private.notify` and the email outbox (`lib/notify/outbox.ts`).

**WHERE** New table `public.rent_acknowledgements` (`rent_payment_id` unique, `mandate_id`, `token_hash`, `answer` in `confirmed|disputed`, `answered_at`, `note` bounded at 400 characters, append-only with no update policy). A service-role writer in `lib/rent/actions.ts`. A new route `app/(site)/confirm/[token]/page.tsx`, open to signed-out visitors like `/price/area/[id]`. The read goes on the tenancy page (V-47) and on `/admin/money` "Tenancy charges" as a column.

**HOW** 1. After `settleBookingCharge` or `pay_booking_from_wallet` returns ok for a booking with a `rent_payments` row, look up an APPROVED mandate for `listing_id`.
2. Mint a random token, store only its sha256, and send the link via the SMS and WhatsApp channel proposed in (V-96, one principal contact surface shared with V-31) and by email if present.
3. The page reads under service role by token and renders the area, the six parts and the dates. The answer is a single insert, and a second answer is refused by a unique constraint.
4. A dispute raises a `risk_alerts` row and pauses the lister's payout of that charge (V-33).
5. With no mandate, the tenancy page says in plain words "No landlord is on record for this listing" (a null is not a negative, per PRODUCT.md section 6).

**WHAT IT BEATS** Asking the tenant to phone the landlord (they cannot: the agent guards the number). Publishing the principal's phone (kills mandate uploads, as the migration argues). Holding the rent in escrow until the landlord confirms: ADR-E1 refuses `first_rent` and it needs a licence. This gets the same protection from **information**, with no custody.

**WHO ELSE HAS IT** Nobody. Classifieds never see the payment. Kwaba and CredPal pay the landlord directly and so know the landlord exists, but they take the rent figure from the tenant and never show the landlord what the tenant was charged. Muster and Rentsmallsmall are the landlord's agent, so the conflict is internal.

**CAN IT BE COPIED** Not by Friday, and not by any classifieds site at all. It needs the mandate record, the frozen transacted figure and the payment, all three on one platform. A competitor needs a verification operation collecting principal contacts (months) and a transaction rail (a year).

### V-36 | MONEY | TRANSFORMATIVE | weeks | DIFFERENTIATOR | The caution register: a deposit that is owed, not held

**WHAT** Every `rent_payments` row with `caution_minor > 0` creates a **caution obligation**: who owes it (the lister, and the principal if acknowledged under V-32), how much in kobo, and from when it is due back (tenancy end, from `move_in + rent_period`, plus a return window the founder sets; 30 days matches the demand-letter practice). The tenancy page shows "Caution ₦560,000 · owed back by 30 Nov 2027". At move-out the lister files deductions as **itemised lines**, each tied to a move-out handover item (V-54) and a photo. Free-text lump sums are refused. The tenant accepts or disputes each line. The return itself is paid **inside Vallo**, lister wallet to tenant wallet, as a wallet-to-wallet transfer carrying `caution_for` metadata, so it lands on the obligation automatically. Each lister's standing then shows a dated, derived fact: "Returned 9 of 10 cautions on time. Average deduction 6 per cent." It is never a badge that can be bought, and it is only shown once there are five or more settled obligations.

**WHY** "The landlord who won't return the deposit" is the most common tenancy grievance in Nigeria, and research 3.2 and RECOMMENDATIONS 3.5 are right that the fight is about **proof, not custody**. They stop at the evidence record (G-1). The missing half is that nobody **records the debt**. A caution paid through Vallo today vanishes into a lister's ledger share with the word "refundable" beside it and no row saying anybody owes anything. The register makes the obligation visible to both sides for 12 months, makes the deductions arguable line by line, and makes the return rate a public reputational asset. In a low-trust market, reputation is the currency that clears (RECOMMENDATIONS 4.6). The draft Lagos bill will require exactly "refund less documented damage"; this is that clause as a product.

**FITS BY** `rent_payments.caution_minor` (already frozen), `transferToUser` in `lib/wallet/actions.ts` (the wallet-to-wallet door, which stays), and the escrow evidence vocabulary pattern (a closed set of facts, not prose) from `lib/escrow/copy.ts` `ESCROW_FACTS`. The standing surface follows `private.sweep_badges`, derived nightly, never granted.

**WHERE** New `public.caution_obligations` (`rent_payment_id` unique, `amount_minor`, `due_at`, `state` in `open|deductions_proposed|agreed|disputed|returned|written_off`, with a transition trigger in the `escrows` style). New `public.caution_deductions` (append-only: `item` from the inspection item enum, `amount_minor`, `photo_id`, `tenant_answer`). The return wallet transfer references `rm-caution-<obligation uuid>`, derived from the row as ADR-E1 4b requires. Routes: the tenancy page (V-47) and `/agent/bookings`.

**HOW** 1. A trigger on `rent_payments` insert opens the obligation.
2. pg_cron sends reminders to the lister at due minus 30 days and due minus 7 days, and to the tenant on the due date.
3. The deduction composer offers only the eight `inspection_report_items.item` values, each needing a move-out photo.
4. Return via a service-role action wrapping the transfer. `sum(returned) + sum(agreed deductions) = amount` is a CHECK on close.
5. A disputed line goes to the existing support desk with both evidence sets. Vallo never moves the lister's money, it records.

**WHAT IT BEATS** Holding the caution in escrow (refused by ADR-E1, fails test 3, twelve-month float, "who gets the interest"). A caution **insurance** product (regulated by NAICOM, and it prices the fraud instead of preventing it). A plain reminder (no leverage).

**WHO ELSE HAS IT** Nobody. Shortlet operators and landlords hold the caution themselves. Rentsmallsmall insures landlords against tenants, the opposite direction. Blogs tell tenants to write demand letters because no system exists.

**CAN IT BE COPIED** Only by a platform that saw the caution paid, knows the tenancy dates and holds both parties' wallets. Classifieds are shut out permanently, and a fintech lender would need the marketplace. It takes a year before the return rates mean anything, and that year is the moat.

### V-92 | MONEY | HIGH | weeks | DIFFERENTIATOR | The shortlet caution is a hold on the guest's own balance, released at checkout

**WHAT** A stay whose host declares a caution (a new `caution_minor` on `rate_plans`, or on the listing for listing-spine stays) collects it **as a hold on the guest's own wallet**, not as a payment to anyone: a PENDING debit in the guest's `wallet_entries`, exactly the shape of a withdrawal hold. **WHY** The "caution fee ambush" and the unreturned shortlet caution are the loudest complaints in the Nigerian stays market (Premium Times, 18 Sept 2026). Research structure E ("a hold placed on a balance already in the user's Vallo wallet, with no new money taken") was judged "the structure to ship first" because it is the least regulatory exposure of all five: the money never leaves the guest in the accounting sense. It was rejected for **agency fees** because the payee is the one who must be protected. For a **caution** it is exactly right, because the guest is the owner and the host only has a contingent claim. The existing decision "escrow does not extend to stays" (RECOMMENDATIONS 0.2) is about custody. This is not custody, and I flag it for the founder rather than smuggle it past.

**FITS BY** `public.hold_wallet_withdrawal` / PENDING debit machinery (`20260809080815…:104-117`) and `private.wallet_spendable_locked` (the hold subtracts inside the lock), `sweepStaleWithdrawalHolds` pattern for the release, the escrow evidence bucket pattern for claims, and `booking_refunds` for the desk's decision record.

**WHERE** A migration adding kinds `caution_hold` and `caution_release`, `rate_plans.caution_minor`, and `bookings.caution_entry_id`. `lib/wallet/ledger.ts`, `app/(app)/checkout/*`, a cron job, `app/admin/bookings`.

**HOW** At payment, write the COMPLETED stay debit and the PENDING caution hold under one lock, with the reference derived from the booking id (`rm-caution-<booking uuid>`). Release at `check_out + 48h` by marking the PENDING entry REVERSED. A claim converts it to COMPLETED plus a transfer credit to the host for the ruled amount, split where needed as two entries. **WHAT IT BEATS** Collecting the caution as payment to the host (the status quo, off-platform). A card pre-authorisation (RECOMMENDATIONS 4.6 explains that Nigerian bank transfers cannot be authorised and voided, and pre-auths lapse in about 7 days). Vallo holding it (licence).

**CAN IT BE COPIED** Only by a platform that is also the guest's wallet. It needs a one-ledger wallet with PENDING-hold semantics, which is what this codebase spent months proving.

### V-34 | SUPPLY | TRANSFORMATIVE | weeks | DIFFERENTIATOR | The Vallo Record: proof an agent is good at the job, counted, dated, and portable

**WHAT** Every lister gets a Record: a short set of counted facts, each with its denominator and window, never combined into a score. ### V-38 | SUPPLY | TRANSFORMATIVE | weeks | DIFFERENTIATOR | The flat remembers: every Vallo let seeds its own successor listing

**WHO ELSE HAS IT** Nobody in Nigeria. NPC and PropertyPro know asking prices

**CAN IT BE COPIED** Not without transacting rent. A competitor would first

first successor listing shows a history.

10 recommendations here, in rank order.

## WHAT WE DID NOT RECOMMEND, AND WHY

Every one of these was proposed by somebody, argued, and dropped. Most of them are tempting, several are what a competitor would do, and a few are what the market will expect Vallo to do. The reasons are the point.

### Money and custody

1. **Hold the caution deposit in escrow for the tenancy.** Twelve months of somebody else's money is deposit-taking by any reading. ADR-E1 refuses `rent_deposit` as a purpose; holding third-party funds sits in the CBN's licensed categories, with capital requirements measured in billions of naira against Vallo's share capital; and it raises "who keeps the interest" against five separate "we charge nothing" promises. V-36 gets the benefit from a recorded obligation, evidence and a public return rate instead, without holding a naira.
2. **Hold first rent until move-in or key handover.** The largest possible float, a trigger nobody can observe (move-in is not an event the platform sees), and the landlord is not a party. V-32 and V-33 protect the rent with information and direct settlement.
3. **Vallo lends: rent now, pay later on its own book.** A money-lending licence and capital, and Spleet's 2024 collapse is the cautionary tale of a platform putting its solvency between tenants and their homes. It would also make "the platform charges nothing" false. V-44 gets the product through a licensed partner inside Vallo's chrome.
4. **Interest, yield or "rent savings with returns" on pots.** Paying a return on customer balances is a regulated deposit or investment activity. `SAVINGS_POTS.md` deliberately makes a rate inexpressible, and that is right.
5. **An inspection or viewing fee, even held.** It breaks "never charge a member to look", it mirrors the commonest scam word for word, and holding ₦5,000 is the same regulated act as holding ₦5m. Reputation clears better (V-34).
6. **Caution insurance or a deposit-replacement product.** NAICOM-regulated, needs an underwriter, and prices the fraud instead of preventing it.
7. **Card pre-authorisation for shortlet cautions.** Nigerian bank transfers cannot be authorised and voided, pre-auths lapse in about a week, and many guests' cards are capped. V-92 holds on the guest's own wallet balance instead, which works on every rail that can fund the wallet.
8. **Paying rent into the wallet and "releasing monthly" to the landlord.** Custody plus an implied credit product: a year of float against one million naira of share capital.
9. **Letting a met release condition pay out early.** The first mechanism in the product where one person's own assertion would move money. Nothing here depends on it.
10. **Capping agency fees on Vallo.** The founder has already said Vallo publishes fees and does not cap them, and a cap would send agents straight to WhatsApp. V-12 prints the ratio beside the law's number, which does a cap's work without the exodus.
11. **Settling stays to the host at the moment of payment.** It kills the arrival-check recourse. For stays, settlement at check-in plus 24 hours is right (V-91); for rent, immediate settlement is right (V-33), because a tenancy has no arrival the platform can observe.
12. **Crypto rent payments.** Price exposure between top-up and charge, anti-money-laundering exposure on a sector SCUML already supervises, and no tenant demand anybody could find. The crypto code should leave the shipped app entirely (V-83).

### Identity, trust and safety

13. **Mandatory NIN for every member.** It would make the member side scarce in one step, and it breaks the founder's rule that a person looking for a home is never asked to verify, puts the most sensitive identifier in Nigeria in front of a stranger who is only browsing, and loses every cautious renter at the door. A confirmed phone (V-50) gets most of the scarcity for none of the cost.
14. **BVN verification.** A CBN-regulated financial identifier, more sensitive than the NIN, a known account-takeover vector, and unnecessary: vNIN plus payout-name matching (V-49) proves the same person without holding a key to their banking. Under NDPA minimisation it would be hard to justify.
15. **A single trust score per agent or listing.** Easy to rank by and impossible to explain. It collapses the five signals `docs/PRODUCT.md` section 6 says must stay separate, and it gets gamed on its cheapest input. V-21 deletes the one that exists.
16. **Publishing the landlord's number, or letting renters call landlords directly.** It would cut out bad agents for a month, and then agents would stop uploading mandates and the check would die of its own success. V-31 gets the landlord's truth without giving anybody his number.
17. **WhatsApp as a messaging channel, bridging Vallo threads into WhatsApp.** The thread would live on Meta's servers, and Vallo would lose the record every dispute, recall, truth answer and collusion check depends on. "Deals made outside are not protected" would become meaningless. WhatsApp is a doorbell, never a room (V-96).
18. **Blocking any message with ten digits in it.** Phone numbers, dates and prices trip it, scammers spell digits out within a day, and the renter learns nothing. Checking the number for the receiver (V-04) turns the scammer's move into evidence.
19. **Showing the resolved account holder's name in chat.** It would make Vallo a free lookup service for any account's owner. Match or no match, rate-limited, protects the renter without that.
20. **Live GPS tracking of renters during inspections.** It reveals the property's address by definition, which no share artefact may carry, costs data and battery on the phones least able to spare either, and stores location history Vallo has no business keeping. V-62 shares the area and the verified person, which is enough to act on.
21. **Paid verification, paid priority review, "Premium" or "Boost" for listers, even at zero price.** A badge that can be bought makes every badge worthless, and a boost is the incumbents' model and the reason their catalogues rot. Supply-side services with a real cost, such as a paid physical inspection, may be charged for; position never (V-06).
22. **A public list of stopped agents.** Defamation exposure on every row, a permanent record of a stop that may later be lifted, and it names people to strangers. V-60 tells exactly the people who talked to the stopped account.
23. **Face-match on every sign-in.** Expensive, fails in poor light on low-end cameras, and turns a routine action into a biometric event with a retention question. The new-device push with a money hold (V-19) protects what is worth taking.
24. **Requiring LASRERA registration to list in Lagos.** Vallo is not the regulator, a large share of real supply is owners and informal agents, and a gate sends them back to WhatsApp where nobody checks anything. V-87 shows it as a dated credential.
25. **Airbnb-style double-blind reviews for rentals.** Tenant and agent are not symmetric after a move-in, and waiting for an agent who never writes would hide the tenant's answer to the door-price question indefinitely. Right for stays, where `MK-59` and `MK-61` own it.
26. **Letting renters review an agent after an enquiry.** High volume, low signal, and it invites extortion ("reduce the fee or I review you"). Reviews stay tied to a transaction; the Record counts behaviour.

### Discovery and supply

27. **Reopening search and listings to signed-out visitors.** The single biggest growth lever, and the founder ruled against it on 23 September in `apps/web/src/proxy.ts`. The brief is to work inside the system, so it is raised as a question in TO THE FOUNDER instead, with V-07 and V-82 as the most public surface compatible with the ruling.
28. **Serving OpenGraph tags to link unfurlers on the gated listing URL.** Quick, and it is cloaking: different content for bots and humans on one URL, broken the day an unfurler changes its user agent, and it publishes listing data on a page humans are refused. The share door does the same job honestly.
29. **Scraping or importing listings from Nigeria Property Centre, PropertyPro or Jiji to seed supply.** Legally exposed, forbidden by `docs/API_INVENTORY.md`, and it would make every trust signal on the platform a lie on the first day. V-09 imports the agent's own words instead.
30. **Commute times from a routing engine.** Free-flow routing is wrong in Lagos by a factor of two to four at peak, and a Google call sends the member's workplace to a third party. A precise wrong number is worse than an honest range (V-43).
31. **An exact-location map with pins on the building.** It reverses the private address, the gate-details-on-confirmation design and the no-address rule for a small gain. The commute answers what people actually use the map for.
32. **A neighbourhood score out of ten.** The trust-score collapse applied to places. Counted, dated resident reports (V-41) say more and claim less.
33. **"12 people are looking at this".** Urgency theatre, a dark pattern, and it pushes renters to pay before inspecting. The count belongs to the lister (V-73).
34. **An AI "best match" percentage on every card.** A number the code cannot explain is a claim it cannot prove. The published ranking is the honest version.
35. **Live video viewings inside the app.** Unreliable on Nigerian mobile data from inside a compound, needs both parties free at once, and the natural fallback, a WhatsApp video call, leaves the chrome. The asynchronous "Show me" clip (V-69) answers the specific doubt.
36. **A separate serviced-apartments market.** Serviced is a property of a rent, not a market. V-68 defines it and derives it.
37. **QR codes on TO LET boards.** Painted QR codes do not scan, need data and a camera at the moment of reading, and cannot be read from a moving car. A code a person can read aloud (V-08) can.

### The platform

38. **An Expo rewrite of the app now.** Safer for 4.2 in the long run, but it doubles the surface before there is one real listing. Six native capabilities in the Capacitor shell are what 4.2 asks for; revisit after the first rejection, not before.
39. **Offline mode for the whole catalogue.** Inventory goes stale in days, megabytes land on 16 GB phones, and it contradicts the service worker's deliberate refusal to cache HTML. Targeted packs for what the person has committed to (V-35, V-77) are the right size.
40. **Showing the naira cost of the data a page used.** Emotionally powerful and a claim the code cannot prove, since prices vary by network and bundle. V-79 shows megabytes, which it can measure.
41. **A home-screen widget with the wallet balance.** Exactly the exposure on a shared or stolen phone that biometrics (V-81) exist to close.
42. **A separate "Vallo Lite" app.** Two products for a team of one. V-79 makes the one product light on demand.
43. **A third-party helpdesk for support.** Claiming and SLAs for free, and every ticket's personal data leaves for a new processor and loses its link to the person file. V-89 builds the four features actually needed.
44. **Storybook for the design system.** The 204 preview pages already are a catalogue; moving them into their own workspace costs a day, and Storybook would cost a month of stories and a second build.
45. **Deleting the four welcome slides.** They slow the first run, but the art and copy are the product's best and slide two teaches the claims rule to every new user. V-18 keeps them for cold arrivals and skips them for anyone who arrived wanting something.
46. **Third-party push services such as OneSignal.** A third party would hold every device token and message text, against the privacy posture in `docs/RETENTION_SCHEDULE.md`. V-53 sends through Vallo's own drain.

## TO THE FOUNDER

Everything the four of us wanted to say to you, since none of it goes in chat.

### Decisions only you can take

1. **Switch rent payment off until V-33 ships, or at least ship its first step today.** The rent charge is live code. It debits the tenant's whole move-in total (rent, caution and fees together), credits nobody, and tells the tenant "the agent has been paid". That is the same custody question as escrow at ten to twenty times the size, and it is not behind a flag. When you write to the solicitor about escrow, ask about the wallet and about a rent charge that settles through Vallo's account to a lister, not only about escrow.
2. **Messaging landlords who never signed up (V-31, V-32).** It needs a consent sentence at every mandate call, an SMS aggregator account and a WhatsApp Business number. It is the best idea in this file and it will cost you some agents. Decide whether a confirmed mandate becomes a ranking input or stays purely a dated fact. And decide whether a landlord saying "I never instructed this agent" stops the agent automatically or only stops the listing and opens an alert; the file says the second, because one landlord in a fee dispute should not be able to stop an agent on his word alone.
3. **The sign-in wall.** Is it permanent, or a launch posture while the catalogue is examples? Today a listing shared on WhatsApp unfurls as "Sign in | Vallo", no listing can be found on Google, and a first-time renter must give an email before seeing a single price. If you read the ruling as "no listing page without an account", a share card (V-07) and public area price pages (V-82) fit it. If you read it as "nothing about any listing", then the Share button should be removed rather than left producing a login card, and marketing spend becomes the only acquisition channel. Either answer is coherent; the current state is not.
4. **The identity vendor.** vNIN, payout-name matching, the ban that follows the person and the collusion graph all queue behind one contract with a NIMC-licensed aggregator. The per-check price is cents. The real decision is who holds your users' identity checks.
5. **"No paid placement, ever", in public (V-06).** It is the clearest positioning line in this file and it binds future revenue. The fee policy already promises never to charge a member to look, save, message or enquire; this adds never selling a lister position.
6. **The renter passport (V-100) and your rule that a member is never asked to verify.** It is written so Vallo never asks and the renter offers. If you read the rule as "Vallo never even offers", cut it and keep phone confirmation, which does not verify identity.
7. **Phone confirmation at three moments (V-50).** It adds friction before a renter's first inspection request. The trade is right, because an inspection costs an agent a Saturday, but it is your funnel.
8. **The shortlet caution as a hold on the guest's own wallet (V-92).** The standing decision says escrow does not extend to stays. A pending hold on the guest's own balance is, on the escrow research's own analysis, not custody. Do you accept that distinction?
9. **The held agency fee as the default (V-56) once held payments open.** Agents will object, and the objection is the point. An opt-out on the listing, visible to tenants, is the compromise.
10. **Who gets the relist prompt (V-38) when an agent holds the mandate.** Mandate holder first with the owner copied keeps agents loyal across tenancies; owner first is the strongest owner-supply mechanism in the file. We lean to the mandate holder.
11. **The Lagos tenancy bill.** When it is signed, decide whether Vallo refuses listings that exceed the new limits or keeps publishing and printing the ratio. We recommend printing the ratio and refusing only what becomes unlawful to demand, such as more than a year upfront from a new tenant.
12. **Taking the phone number off TO LET boards (V-08).** Agents will resist; owners listing directly will not. It could start as a condition of the owner workspace.
13. **The 3-to-5-business-day refund promise.** It is yours and it sits on the Send page, where nothing is refunded, contradicting "usually within minutes" in the stays copy. Pick one number and let V-24 measure it.
14. **Crypto.** The route is dark by your ruling, "deferred, not cancelled", and the 2,959 lines behind it, plus a live `/api/crypto/*`, still ship in the binary a reviewer can open. We recommend a branch until you decide it ships (V-83).
15. **Moving the server region to Dublin (V-16).** One line and a large speed gain. Confirm first that Paystack and Yellow Card do not allowlist US egress addresses for you.
16. **Resident pulses (V-41).** The platform would ask members a question up to twice a week. Are you comfortable with Vallo asking its members for information, and at what frequency does it become noise?

### Concerns

- **The dead brand is the most visible string on the platform.** Every one of the 64 example cards says "Listed by RentMe Example Collection, agent". It is data, not code, so the i18n sweeps never find it, and it is on every screenshot a store reviewer will take.
- **The example photography trains the eye wrong.** A Yaba mini flat is shown as a lit villa and a Lekki terrace as a hotel suite over a skyline. They are labelled, but they set an expectation that real phone photos of real Nigerian flats will never meet. Modest, honest photos on the rent examples would make the first real listing look normal instead of shabby.
- **The listing page leads with the rent and puts the move-in total second**, although the card and the product rule lead with the total. It is the product's own differentiator put in second place.
- **Verification rests on photographs of documents today.** Until the vNIN rung exists, "identity seen" is honest and "verified" in any renter-facing sentence overstates it.
- **The address rung asks for a utility bill in the applicant's name**, which most Nigerian tenants, including agents who rent their own homes, do not have because the bill is in the landlord's name. Expect good applicants to fail there. A NIMC record address, a tenancy agreement (already accepted) or the in-person rung are better proofs.
- **Member profiles publish occupation and home town by default.** On the walk, the QA member's occupation and home town were public on their page. That helps a scammer write a script and helps nobody rent a flat (V-64).
- **Nobody is watching.** The catalogue outage was found by you. V-01 and V-51 are small and belong ahead of every feature in this file.
- **Deploy frequency costs your users data.** About fifty commits reached `main` on 23 September. Each production deploy changes the cache key on every asset, so a returning user re-downloads about 450 KB of unchanged JavaScript. V-78 fixes the service worker half; batching production deploys to a few a day fixes the rest.
- **The gate ruling and the store.** With everything behind sign-in, a reviewer sees a slideshow, a sign-in form and then 64 labelled examples. That is honest and allowed, and it is also what 4.2 reviewers call a website in a wrapper unless the native pieces are visible in the first minute. V-11 is the cheapest and most important of them.
- **Sessions never fold.** After a day of QA sign-ins the member account lists 44 or 45 live sessions, each reading "Device not recorded". A devices screen that cannot help a person spot a stranger has lost its only job (V-19).

### Where we disagree with something in the repository

- **`wallet_pots.balance_minor` is a balance column.** The money model says no balance columns. `SAVINGS_POTS.md` defends it with a lock and a guard trigger, and the invariants are real, but it is the one place the rule is broken, and the pot balance can be derived from `pot_hold` minus `pot_release` in the one ledger. Drop it before pots have users.
- **What makes somebody keep money in the wallet.** We think the goal is that money passes through with a perfect record, not that it rests. Balances at rest are the deposit-taking question in its purest form. The wallet recommendations here (V-84, V-93, V-86) give it jobs measured in days: a flatmate's share, a held fee, a caution hold. Resting balances are a licence conversation first.
- **`docs/BADGES.md` treats the Guardian badge as good citizenship.** A public badge for reporting tells a small estate who does the reporting, the first thing a reported person wants to know (V-27).
- **`lib/messages/education.ts` warns the sender.** The words are right and the placement is backwards; the person who needs them is the one receiving an account number (V-04).
- **The report sheet promises 24 hours and `/standards` promises 4** for payment steering. Keep the 4 and make the sheet say it (V-63).
